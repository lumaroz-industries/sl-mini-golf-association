Every article starts with a short, strong opening paragraph. It is shown a little larger than the rest of the text, so use it to tell the reader why this story matters.

Write the rest in plain paragraphs. Keep them short — two to four sentences reads best on a phone.

## Headings organise the story

Use `##` for main sections and `###` for smaller ones. When an article has three or more main sections, a contents list appears next to the text automatically.

### Lists

- Use a hyphen for bullet points.
- Keep each point to one idea.
- Numbered lists work too — start lines with `1.`, `2.`, `3.`

## Photos and galleries

Put a photo on its own line. The text in quotes becomes its caption:

![The illuminated tunnel on the homepage course](/articles/article-template/tunnel.webp "Captions sit under the photo. Credit the photographer here if needed.")

Put several photos together, with no blank line between them, to make a gallery:

![The rotating gate](/articles/article-template/gate.webp) ![The whole course from above](/articles/article-template/course.webp)

Always write a short description inside the square brackets — it is read aloud to people using screen readers.

## Quotes

Start a line with `>` to highlight something a player said:

> Every putt is a small journey. The course just makes it visible.

## Video and Instagram posts

Paste a YouTube or Instagram post link on its own line and it becomes a player that loads only when the reader taps it:

```
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID
https://www.instagram.com/p/POST_ID/
```

## Before you publish

1. Check names, dates and results with the people involved.
2. Make sure you have permission to use every photo.
3. Set `draft: false`, then publish.

That's it — the page layout, sharing buttons and link previews are created automatically.
