Twenty players. One flag. When the Minigolf Open Asian Championships 2026 teed off in Pran Buri, Thailand, India arrived with one of its strongest statements yet: a 20-member squad ready to take on the best of Asia.

For Indian mini golf, this is more than a tournament entry. It is proof of how far the sport has travelled — from local courses and state competitions to an international stage, with the tricolour on every shirt.

![The Indian team, players kneeling with their putters in front and officials and coaches standing behind, at the welcome banner of the 2026 Minigolf Open Asian Championships in Pranpuri, Thailand](/articles/team-india-minigolf-open-asian-championships-2026/team-india.webp "Team India at the welcome banner of the 2026 Minigolf Open Asian Championships, Pranpuri, Thailand.")

## India on the Asian stage

The Minigolf Open Asian Championships bring together the region's finest players for a test of precision, nerve and consistency. Every lane counts, every stroke is recorded, and one missed putt can change a leaderboard.

India's players competed across four formats:

- **Team** — the squad playing together, where every player's score shapes the nation's result.
- **Individual** — each player against the course and the field.
- **Youth** — the next generation, measured against Asia's best young talent.
- **Mixed pairs** — two players, one shared score, and complete trust between them.

> Twenty players, four formats, one flag — Team India in Pran Buri.

## The team behind the team

The Indian contingent competed under the supervision of **Dr Surajsingh Yeotikar**, Secretary General of the [Minigolf Federation of India](/officials), with coaches **Mr Ashish Bhakare** and **Mr Yajvendar Singh Yadav** guiding the players through preparation and competition.

Behind every putt at an international championship is a lot of work that never makes the scorecard: training, travel, selection and the steady presence of the people who keep a squad focused far from home.

## Why this matters

Every international appearance builds something bigger than a result. It gives young players a goal they can see, it puts Indian mini golf in front of the wider Asian community, and it shows that the sport here is ready for the world stage.

Twenty players stood on the greens of Pran Buri for India. The next generation is already watching — and practising.
